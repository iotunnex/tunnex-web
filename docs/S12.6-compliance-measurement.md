# S12.6 — compliance. The measurement, not the ruling.

⛔ **NO LEGAL CONCLUSIONS HERE.** This paper says WHAT THE DATA IS, so a lawyer can rule on it in one
sitting instead of discovering the surface themselves. Every "should" below is a **held decide-item**.

⭐ **The structural fact first, because it is the whole compliance position:** the product's audit logs,
device records, policies and user accounts live in the CUSTOMER'S OWN DEPLOYMENT. Tunnex holds none of it —
there is no phone-home, and licences verify offline. What we hold is the sales funnel and the key ledger,
and it lives in one Cloudflare D1 database attached to this site.

---

## What we actually hold

Seven tables. Measured from `migrations/`, not from memory.

| table                  | personal data                                                             | non-personal                                            | rows expire?                                                                                                                |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `subscribers`          | **email**                                                                 | confirm-token hash, timestamps                          | ⛔ never                                                                                                                    |
| `trial_requests`       | **email**, company **domain**                                             | token hash, expiry, consumed_at                         | ⛔ never — `expires_at` bounds the TOKEN, not the row                                                                       |
| `trials`               | **email**, company **domain**                                             | status, licence id, clock                               | ⛔ never                                                                                                                    |
| `enterprise_leads`     | **name**, **email**, **company**, free-text **message**                   | seats                                                   | ⛔ never                                                                                                                    |
| `licence_review_queue` | **contact email**, **company**, company **domain**, free-text **notes**   | band, term, gateways, decision                          | ⛔ never — decided rows are retained _deliberately_ (0003: "a refusal we cannot explain later is worse than a row we kept") |
| `issued_keys`          | company **domain**, ⚠ **`issued_by`** — the Tunnex operator who signed it | licence id, band, kid, dates, **the signed key itself** | ⛔ never, and see below                                                                                                     |
| `email_events`         | — (joins `trials`)                                                        | kind, sent_at                                           | ⛔ never                                                                                                                    |

### ⚠ Three things worth a lawyer's attention, stated flatly

1. **Two free-text fields accept anything a human types.** `enterprise_leads.message` (2 000 chars) and
   `licence_review_queue.notes`. Nothing constrains what a prospect puts there. Every other column is a
   known shape; these two are not, and a retention rule that assumes "we only hold emails and domains" is
   wrong about exactly these.

2. **`issued_by` makes our own staff data subjects.** S12.10 records the verified Access email of whoever
   signed each key. That is the point — it is the only way to answer "on whose authority did this leave" —
   and it also means the ledger contains employee data, not only customer data.

3. **A company domain is not obviously personal data and not obviously impersonal.** `acme.com` identifies
   an organization; `contact@acme.com` identifies a person; a one-person company's domain arguably does
   both. Named because a rule written for "emails" will silently not cover the domain columns.

## What the site currently promises

`/privacy`, section _"Keeping and deleting your data"_, verbatim:

> We keep the little we collect only as long as it's useful for the purpose you gave it — answering you,
> or sending what you signed up for. To see what we hold or ask us to delete it, email support@tunnex.io
> and a human will handle it.

⛔ **NOTHING IMPLEMENTS EITHER HALF.** Measured: there is no `DELETE` statement anywhere in the codebase
outside of tests, no scheduled purge, no retention job, and no per-subject lookup. Retention in practice is
**indefinite**, and "a human will handle it" is a person reading SQL by hand with no procedure to follow.

⚠ The sentence is not false — a human _would_ handle it — but it describes an intention where a reader
would reasonably infer a mechanism.

## The held decide-items

> ⛔ **Every one of these is a founder or lawyer ruling. None is decided here, and none should be inferred
> from the fact that I measured it.**

**1. Retention periods, per table.** The four funnel tables (`subscribers`, `trial_requests`, `trials`,
`enterprise_leads`) hold data whose purpose ends — an unconsumed trial token is useless after 30 minutes; a
lead that went nowhere three years ago is not answering anyone. A period plus a purge job is buildable in a
day once the numbers are ruled.

**2. Deletion requests, and the one place they collide with something load-bearing.** `subscribers`,
`trial_requests`, `enterprise_leads` can be deleted on request with no consequence. **`issued_keys` cannot**
— it is the ONLY record of what we put into the world, offline verification means we can never ask a
deployment what it is running, and a key someone shows us can only be checked against the set we minted.
⭐ _Deleting the ledger row does not stop the key working; it stops us being able to say whether it is ours._
That tension is a legal question about lawful basis and legitimate interest, not an engineering one.

**3. Where the data physically is.** Cloudflare D1 — I did not measure the region, and the account's D1
placement is a dashboard fact, not a repo fact. It matters for the DPDP/state-privacy residency question
that PLAN.md's S12.6 already names.

**4. Whether `issued_by` (staff email) belongs under the same policy as customer data**, or is employment
data with a different basis and a different retention answer.

**5. Sub-processors, unmeasured and worth listing before a lawyer asks:** Cloudflare (hosting, D1, Access),
the transactional email provider, Turnstile. Each sees some subset of the above.

## What this paper is not

⛔ Not a DPDP or state-privacy assessment · not a decision that any current practice is compliant or is not
· not a retention policy. It is the inventory a lawyer needs so the ruling takes one sitting.

⚠ **And the inventory ages.** Any new column that holds a name, an address, or free text belongs in the
table above. A compliance paper that silently stops matching the schema is worse than none, because the
next reader trusts it.
